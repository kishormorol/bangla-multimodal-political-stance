\begin{abstract}
Evaluating political stance in Bangla news requires a defined stance target and comparable model inputs. We examine a collection of 198 news items with three government stance labels. A provenance audit identifies 145 items with consistent matches to BanglaBias after one verified URL correction, and 53 unmatched items. Separate text and image stance fields agree on 72 of 152 jointly labelled items. Earlier experiments contain augmentation leakage, test informed checkpoint selection, and unequal evaluation populations. The committed matched report compares thirteen models on 149 items with readable images, headline inputs, shared outer folds, and separate validation for checkpoint selection. Its character ngram classifier has the highest macro F1, 0.543. A separate five seed summary reports a mean of 0.562 for that classifier, but all of its paired comparisons record 86 items. The surviving artifacts do not establish the relationship between those input snapshots. The tested vision and language systems score below the character baseline in both summaries. Their differences from BanglaBERT are uncertain or negative, depending on the model and seed. Because text representations also differ, these comparisons do not isolate the contribution of photographs. % sources: reports/submission/matched.json, seeds.json, provenance.json, audit.json; scripts/verify_reports.py
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
A headline and its accompanying photograph may frame the same political event differently. Predicting that framing requires a defined stance target, a clear account of annotation evidence, and a comparison that holds the evaluation population constant. We study individual news items labelled as government-critique, neutral, or government-leaning. These categories describe annotated framing; they do not determine factual truth, misinformation, or an outlet's enduring political orientation.

Bangla political stance resources already exist. In particular, BanglaBias contains 200 news articles with the same three categories \citep{lia2025read}. Our working collection incorporates items from that resource and includes additional image metadata and modality-specific label fields. Its contribution must therefore be distinguished from the original collection and article annotations.

The available model experiments span several evaluation protocols. The original text split was drawn after augmentation, while original baseline vision--language models and fusion models were evaluated on different populations. A later implementation introduced pooled cross-validation scores but used the outer test fold to select neural checkpoints. These issues affect the interpretation of model rankings and gains.

This paper presents a provenance audit, a descriptive analysis of annotation coverage and modality label disagreement, an audit of the experimental lineage, and a matched comparison of thirteen models. Historical model summaries are kept in an appendix for traceability. We do not claim a new first dataset. The matched comparison favors the character baseline among the tested configurations, but does not isolate the effect of adding an image.

\section{Related Work}
\paragraph{Bangla political stance.}
\citet{lia2025read} introduce BanglaBias and evaluate political stance at article level. This resource is part of the lineage of the present collection, rather than solely a comparator. Reusing an article label with only its headline as model input evaluates prediction of that label from a shorter view; it does not establish independently annotated headline-level ground truth. Political stance detection has also been studied in Hindi news \citep{agrawal2022hindi}.

\paragraph{Bangla and multimodal representations.}
BanglaBERT provides a monolingual pretrained representation for Bangla language understanding \citep{bhattacharjee2022banglabert}. Vision--language models offer a possible route to combining a headline and photograph, but the presence of multiple modalities does not establish their incremental usefulness. Multimodal fake-news research surveys cross-modal interaction methods \citep{li2025survey}; our stance target is distinct from veracity detection. We evaluate the conditions needed to compare text-only and multimodal systems on this collection.

Annotators~2 and~3 each cover 197 items and have Cohen's $\kappa=0.731$ on their 197-item overlap. \subsection{Text and image availability}
The original preprocessed text consists of short headlines, with a median of eight words. The current processed text column mixes 127 recovered article bodies and 71 headlines. The primary corrected comparison fixes the input to the headline field for every model, avoiding source-dependent article-recovery differences. Until annotation records specify otherwise, this is described as predicting item stance from headlines rather than newly annotating headline stance.

The current corpus has 150 image-path entries, of which 149 decode successfully in the audited snapshot. Earlier vision--language summaries use 86 items, while a restricted re-score of text predictions uses 87. These figures refer to different snapshots or eligibility rules; they are not interchangeable descriptions of the dataset. The corrected comparison freezes the 149 decodable items as its primary population. Image loading converts readable files to RGB and resizes according to model configuration. The present artifacts do not establish perceptual-hash deduplication, a minimum-resolution exclusion rule, or aspect-ratio-preserving padding for every run.

\section{Methods and Evaluation Lineage}\label{sec:methods}
\subsection{Model families}
The comparison includes five text encoders: BanglaBERT \citep{bhattacharjee2022banglabert}, a community Bangla-ELECTRA checkpoint (\texttt{monsoon-nlp/bangla-electra}; architecture from \citealp{clark2020electra}), mBERT \citep{devlin2019bert}, XLM-RoBERTa \citep{conneau2020xlmr}, and the mT5 encoder with a classification head \citep{xue2021mt5}. It also includes a TF-IDF/logistic-regression baseline and a majority baseline. The text baseline uses character-within-word 2--5-grams, minimum document frequency~2, up to 50,000 features, and logistic regression with $C=1$ and balanced class weights. Vectorization is fitted on training data. The majority floor uses original training-label frequencies without augmentation.

The vision--language families are CLIP \citep{radford2021clip}, ALIGN \citep{jia2021align}, BLIP \citep{li2022blip}, ViLT \citep{kim2021vilt}, FLAVA \citep{singh2022flava}, and a CountVectorizer/ViT hybrid. In the inspected implementation, the hybrid concatenates count-vector text features with frozen ViT representations and fits logistic regression, rather than the SVM described in the previous manuscript. Optimizers, encoder freezing, image sizes, learning rates, and epoch budgets vary by configuration; a single training recipe does not describe all models. For example, the saved BanglaBERT configuration specifies eight epochs, batch size~16, learning rate $2\times10^{-5}$, and early-stopping patience~3.

\subsection{Historical evaluation protocols}
The original text predictions contain 47 test rows from a fixed split of the augmented corpus. Of these, 20 are augmented rows, and 25 source articles are shared between training and test. Thus, the original text scores cannot establish generalization to unseen source articles. Original baseline vision--language predictions cover 30 items from a fixed split, while the original proposed fusion notebook uses five folds over 149 image-associated items. An accuracy of 63.2\% from that fusion notebook cannot establish superiority over a baseline scored on a separate 30-item split.

A later cross-validation implementation reports pooled out-of-fold predictions and fold-level variation. However, its training wrapper supplies the outer test fold as both validation and test data. The neural trainer selects the best checkpoint using validation macro-F1, so this exposes the outer evaluation labels to checkpoint selection. Its grouping also falls back to individual item IDs when the requested source-group column is absent. The corpus lacks that column, and individual item IDs do not protect the repeated source URLs. We have repaired the shared split logic to join declared groups, normalized URLs, and augmentation parents transitively; Tables~\ref{tab:text_models} and~\ref{tab:multimodal_models} in Appendix~\ref{sec:historical} retain the historical summaries; they are not corrected-protocol results.

\subsection{Corrected comparison protocol}\label{sec:experimental_setup}
The required primary comparison uses the same original readable-image items, headline inputs, source groups, and outer folds for every text-only and multimodal model. Repeated source URLs must share a group; metadata conflicts require correction or conservative exclusion before the item list is frozen. Evaluation retains the natural label distribution.

Within each outer training partition, a separate grouped validation partition selects neural checkpoints. The outer test partition is used only for final prediction. Any augmented headline is attached only if its parent belongs to the inner training partition. The published augmentation table contains 114 variants; neither validation nor test data should contain these variants. Section~\ref{sec:results} reports the experiment run under this specification.

The runner asserts that every eligible original item receives exactly one prediction from an outer test fold. The committed matched summary retains fold item IDs, configurations, seeds, and an input checksum, but its code revision fields are null. The original prediction files and training logs are not committed. The matched summary records 149 items with readable images and common outer folds for every model. A secondary headline evaluation covers all 198 items for text models and is reported separately. The input checksum does not identify image bytes or software versions. % source: reports/submission/matched.json

Configurations are committed with the implementation. Text encoders are trained for up to eight epochs. Vision and language backbones are frozen, with a trained fusion or classification head for up to ten epochs. Neural models use class weights and early stopping with patience~3 on validation macro F1. The CountVectorizer/ViT hybrid uses frozen ViT features \citep{dosovitskiy2021vit} and logistic regression. The matched summary uses seed~42. A separate summary covers seeds~42 to~46, but its paired comparisons record 86 items rather than 149. We therefore report it separately. The seed controls both fold assignment and model initialisation, so variation across seeds cannot separate those two sources. % sources: configs/, reports/submission/matched.json, seeds.json

\subsection{Metrics and uncertainty}
The primary metric is macro-F1 over the three fixed labels. We report accuracy, per-class F1, pooled macro-F1, an item-level percentile bootstrap interval with 2,000 resamples, and the mean and sample standard deviation of fold macro-F1. Pooled F1 is not an arithmetic mean of fold F1. The bootstrap conditions on saved predictions; it does not measure retraining variability or label uncertainty. A corrected analysis should account for repeated source groups when resampling.

Multimodal improvement is assessed with paired predictions for identical items under identical outer folds. We fixed the comparisons before seeing results: each vision--language model against two text references, TF-IDF/logistic regression and BanglaBERT. For each pair we report the difference in pooled macro-F1 and a percentile interval from 2,000 bootstrap resamples of source groups rather than items, so items sharing a source are resampled together. A separate paired analysis covers 86 items at each of five seeds; we report how many seeds yield a bootstrap interval entirely above or below zero. Outlet- or event-held-out evaluation is not performed.

\subsection{Relation to historical scores}
The historical summary reported BanglaBERT at 0.533 on 198 items (Table~\ref{tab:text_models}); the corrected run gives 0.366. % source: reports/submission/matched.json
The two runs differ in several ways at once. The corrected run selects checkpoints without the outer test fold, trains on roughly 80\% of each outer training partition, and fixes the input to headlines with repaired source groups. We do not attribute the drop to any single change. Inner validation partitions contain 25 items on the matched population and 33 on all headlines. Early stopping ended 65 of 75 neural fold runs. Among those 65, 29 selected checkpoints from epoch 1 or 2; the summary does not record stopping at those epochs. % source: reports/submission/early_stopping.json, matched.json folds
Early checkpoint selection could reflect noisy validation, overfitting, or insufficient learning. The saved summary does not distinguish those explanations.

\section{Discussion}
The audit changes what the evidence can support. First, direct overlap with BanglaBias requires attribution and a clear distinction between inherited article data and any additional image collection or annotations. The 53 unmatched rows and annotation procedures still require documentation before a resource-novelty claim.

Second, the character baseline has the highest saved score in the matched report and at every seed in the separate seed summary. The latter cannot currently establish replication on the 149 item population. Comparisons with BanglaBERT include uncertainty or negative differences, depending on the system and seed. Possible explanations include text representation, training budget, checkpoint selection, and limited data. The present comparisons do not separate them. Agreement of 72 out of 152 modality labels describes stored judgments; disagreement alone does not show that images improve prediction of the final label. A matched text representation with and without image features would address that separate question. % sources: reports/submission/matched.json, seeds.json, audit.json

Third, a common cross-validation label is insufficient to establish comparability. Every historical text score is higher than its corrected all-headline counterpart (Tables~\ref{tab:text_models} and~\ref{tab:all_headlines}). Removing test-informed checkpoint selection and equalizing populations changed both the absolute scores and the ranking, with a linear baseline moving from second place to first.

Finally, claims that tokenizers render Bangla text unusable, photographs are merely decorative, or a particular fusion architecture explains a gain would require direct diagnostics or controlled ablations. The historical zero-shot LLM summary lacks a matched item population and verified response provenance; it is omitted.

\section{Conclusion}
The collection contains reused BanglaBias items, uneven annotation coverage, and separate modality labels. The historical evaluation contains leakage, test informed checkpoint selection, and unequal populations. The committed matched report compares thirteen models on 149 items and favors a character headline classifier. The separate five seed comparison records 86 items and also favors that baseline among the tested systems. Missing run artifacts prevent reconciliation of the two input snapshots. The available comparisons change text representation as well as image use, so they cannot establish the effect of photographs. % sources: reports/submission/matched.json, seeds.json, provenance.json, audit.json

\section*{Limitations}
The sample is small and imbalanced, with 39 outlet keys and uneven image availability. The seed changes both folds and initialisation, so those sources of variation cannot be separated. Repeated seeds do not add independent news items. Other training settings could change individual scores, but no controlled diagnostic identifies their effect here. The paired comparisons change text representation as well as image use. Original prediction files, training logs, exact software versions, and seed input membership are unavailable in the committed reports. The BLIP wrapper selects a text state from a backbone whose ordinary forward pass computes text and image separately; its historical score therefore does not demonstrate image conditioned classification. A corrected feature path requires a separately identified experiment. Exact lineage for unmatched items, the stance target in different political periods, image collection, annotation instructions, adjudication, consent, and compensation need original study records. The removal of two raw items and several metadata exceptions remain unresolved. % code: src/bmpb/models/multimodal.py at a935d11; upstream transformers v4.40.0 BlipModel.forward

Source-URL grouping alone does not eliminate shared event, outlet, or visual information. Readable-image availability can be selective. Item-bootstrap intervals in Tables~\ref{tab:matched} and~\ref{tab:all_headlines} do not represent training or annotation uncertainty. Outlet- or event-held-out evaluation was not performed.

\section*{Ethical Considerations}
The labels concern annotated framing of individual items, not factual correctness or a normative assessment of a publisher. Political stance is context-sensitive, and disagreement should be retained and documented rather than hidden by an assumed adjudication rule. The measured pairwise agreement does not establish representative annotator viewpoints or absence of bias.

The upstream BanglaBias dataset must be credited. Its dataset card lists an MIT license, but that does not establish permissions for added third-party photographs, recovered article content, or new annotations. Any release should distinguish code, annotations, source links, and copyrighted content, and document their respective terms. We do not claim that publication of news content establishes consent or unrestricted reuse. Automated stance predictions should not be treated as evidence sufficient for censorship, political targeting, or publisher sanctions.
