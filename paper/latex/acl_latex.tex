\begin{abstract}
Evaluating political stance in Bangla news requires distinguishing item-level framing from outlet-level bias and controlling the evidence available to models. We examine a collection of 198 news items with government-critique, neutral, and government-leaning labels. A provenance audit identifies 145 items with consistent headline or source-link matches to BanglaBias after one verified URL correction, and 53 unmatched items. Separate text and image stance fields agree on 72 of 152 jointly labelled items. Auditing the earlier experiments, we find augmentation leakage in the original text split, test-fold checkpoint selection in a later cross-validation implementation, and unequal evaluation populations. We then rerun thirteen text and vision--language models under a corrected protocol: a source-grouped five-fold split per seed of 149 readable-image items, headline input for every model, and checkpoint selection on an inner validation split. Across five seeds (42--46), a character n-gram TF-IDF classifier averages macro-F1 0.524 (SD 0.021, range 0.497--0.543). % source: reports/submission/seeds.json
Every vision--language model scores below it at every seed: mean paired differences range from $-0.102$ to $-0.224$, and source-group bootstrap intervals exclude zero in 3--5 of 5 seeds. Against fine-tuned BanglaBERT (mean 0.358, SD 0.036), CLIP is higher on average ($+0.064$), with an interval entirely above zero in 2 of 5 seeds; the other vision--language models are not reliably different from it. Image ablations show that only CLIP uses the photograph: replacing it with a blank image lowers CLIP's macro-F1 by 0.168 on average, with intervals above zero in all five seeds, while the other models are unaffected. % source: reports/submission/ablation.json
Even with the photograph, CLIP stays below the headline-only TF-IDF classifier. On this sample, no tested multimodal model outperforms the best headline-only classifier.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
A headline and its accompanying photograph may frame the same political event differently. Predicting that framing requires a defined stance target, a clear account of annotation evidence, and a comparison that holds the evaluation population constant. We study individual news items labelled as government-critique, neutral, or government-leaning. These categories describe annotated framing; they do not determine factual truth, misinformation, or an outlet's enduring political orientation.

Bangla political stance resources already exist. In particular, BanglaBias contains 200 news articles with the same three categories \citep{lia2025read}. Our working collection incorporates items from that resource and includes additional image metadata and modality-specific label fields. Its contribution must therefore be distinguished from the original collection and article annotations.

The available model experiments span several evaluation protocols. The original text split was drawn after augmentation, while original baseline vision--language models and fusion models were evaluated on different populations. A later implementation introduced pooled cross-validation scores but used the outer test fold to select neural checkpoints. These issues affect the interpretation of model rankings and gains.

This paper makes four contributions: a row-level provenance audit, a descriptive analysis of annotation coverage and modality-label disagreement, an audit of the experimental lineage, and a matched re-evaluation of thirteen text and vision--language models under one corrected protocol. Historical model summaries are kept in an appendix for traceability. We do not claim a new first-of-its-kind dataset. In the corrected comparison, no multimodal model outperforms the best headline-only classifier, and only one of six uses the photograph measurably.

\section{Related Work}
\paragraph{Bangla political stance.}
\citet{lia2025read} introduce BanglaBias and evaluate political stance at article level. This resource is part of the lineage of the present collection, rather than solely a comparator. Reusing an article label with only its headline as model input evaluates prediction of that label from a shorter view; it does not establish independently annotated headline-level ground truth. Political stance detection has also been studied in Hindi news \citep{agrawal2022hindi}.

\paragraph{Bangla and multimodal representations.}
BanglaBERT provides a monolingual pretrained representation for Bangla language understanding \citep{bhattacharjee2022banglabert}. Vision--language models offer a possible route to combining a headline and photograph, but the presence of multiple modalities does not establish their incremental usefulness. Multimodal fake-news research surveys cross-modal interaction methods \citep{li2025survey}; our stance target is distinct from veracity detection. We evaluate the conditions needed to compare text-only and multimodal systems on this collection.

\subsection{Text and image availability}
The original preprocessed text consists of short headlines, with a median of eight words. The current processed text column mixes 127 recovered article bodies and 71 headlines. The primary corrected comparison fixes the input to the headline field for every model, avoiding source-dependent article-recovery differences. Until annotation records specify otherwise, this is described as predicting item stance from headlines rather than newly annotating headline stance.

The current corpus has 150 image-path entries, of which 149 decode successfully in the audited snapshot. Earlier vision--language summaries use 86 items, while a restricted re-score of text predictions uses 87. These figures refer to different snapshots or eligibility rules; they are not interchangeable descriptions of the dataset. The corrected comparison freezes the 149 decodable items as its primary population. Image loading converts readable files to RGB and resizes according to model configuration. The present artifacts do not establish perceptual-hash deduplication, a minimum-resolution exclusion rule, or aspect-ratio-preserving padding for every run.

\section{Methods and Evaluation Lineage}\label{sec:methods}
\subsection{Model families}
The comparison includes five text encoders: BanglaBERT \citep{bhattacharjee2022banglabert}, a community Bangla-ELECTRA checkpoint (\texttt{monsoon-nlp/bangla-electra}; architecture from \citealp{clark2020electra}), mBERT \citep{devlin2019bert}, XLM-RoBERTa \citep{conneau2020xlmr}, and the mT5 encoder with a classification head \citep{xue2021mt5}. It also includes a TF-IDF/logistic-regression baseline and a majority baseline. The text baseline uses character-within-word 2--5-grams, minimum document frequency~2, up to 50,000 features, and logistic regression with $C=1$ and balanced class weights. Vectorization is fitted on training data. The majority floor uses original training-label frequencies without augmentation.

The vision--language families are CLIP \citep{radford2021clip}, ALIGN \citep{jia2021align}, BLIP \citep{li2022blip}, ViLT \citep{kim2021vilt}, FLAVA \citep{singh2022flava}, and a CountVectorizer/ViT hybrid. In the inspected implementation, the hybrid concatenates count-vector text features with frozen ViT representations and fits logistic regression, rather than the SVM described in the previous manuscript. Optimizers, encoder freezing, image sizes, learning rates, and epoch budgets vary by configuration; a single training recipe does not describe all models. For example, the saved BanglaBERT configuration specifies eight epochs, batch size~16, learning rate $2\times10^{-5}$, and early-stopping patience~3.

\subsection{Historical evaluation protocols}
The original text predictions contain 47 test rows from a fixed split of the augmented corpus. Of these, 20 are augmented rows, and 25 source articles are shared between training and test. Thus, the original text scores cannot establish generalization to unseen source articles. Original baseline vision--language predictions cover 30 items from a fixed split, while the original proposed fusion notebook uses five folds over 149 image-associated items. An accuracy of 63.2\% from that fusion notebook cannot establish superiority over a baseline scored on a separate 30-item split.

A later cross-validation implementation reports pooled out-of-fold predictions and fold-level variation. However, its training wrapper supplies the outer test fold as both validation and test data. The neural trainer selects the best checkpoint using validation macro-F1, so this exposes the outer evaluation labels to checkpoint selection. Its grouping also falls back to individual item IDs when the requested source-group column is absent. The corpus lacks that column, and individual item IDs do not protect the repeated source URLs. We have repaired the shared split logic to join declared groups, normalized URLs, and augmentation parents transitively; Tables~\ref{tab:text_models} and~\ref{tab:multimodal_models} in Appendix~\ref{sec:historical} retain the historical summaries; they are not corrected-protocol results.

\subsection{Corrected comparison protocol}\label{sec:experimental_setup}
The required primary comparison uses the same original readable-image items, headline inputs, source groups, and outer folds for every text-only and multimodal model. Repeated source URLs must share a group; metadata conflicts require correction or conservative exclusion before the item list is frozen. Evaluation retains the natural label distribution.

Within each outer training partition, a separate grouped validation partition selects neural checkpoints. The outer test partition is used only for final prediction. Any augmented headline is attached only if its parent belongs to the inner training partition. The published augmentation table contains 114 variants; neither validation nor test data should contain these variants. Section~\ref{sec:results} reports the experiment run under this specification.

Every eligible original item receives exactly one out-of-fold prediction, which the runner asserts. Runs retain item IDs, fold assignments, model configuration, seed, code revision, and an input checksum. The primary population is the 149 items whose image decodes; every text and vision--language model sees the same headlines, source groups, and outer folds. A secondary all-headline evaluation covers all 198 items for text models and is reported in a separate table.

Configurations are those committed before the rerun and were not tuned on these results. Text encoders are fine-tuned for up to eight epochs. Vision--language backbones are frozen, with a trained fusion or classification head for up to ten epochs. All neural models use class-weighted cross-entropy and early stopping with patience~3 on inner-validation macro-F1. The CountVectorizer/ViT hybrid uses frozen ViT features \citep{dosovitskiy2021vit} and logistic regression. BLIP is classified from the [CLS] state of its image--text matching encoder, in which the text cross-attends to the image. Each configuration is run with five seeds (42--46) on a single NVIDIA GPU. The seed controls both the fold assignment and model initialisation; within one seed every model shares the same folds.

\paragraph{Image ablations.} To test whether a model uses the photograph, each vision--language model is rerun with every photograph replaced by one uniform grey image (\emph{blank}) and with each item given another item's photograph (\emph{shuffled}, a seeded derangement), all else fixed. Two further controls are fixed in advance: TF-IDF+ViT, which concatenates the exact TF-IDF features of the text baseline with frozen ViT features in the same logistic regression, compared with TF-IDF alone and with its shuffled-photo version; and ViT-only, compared with the majority floor. Each ablation is compared with its unablated model on identical items and folds, using the same source-group bootstrap and five seeds.

\subsection{Metrics and uncertainty}
The primary metric is macro-F1 over the three fixed labels. We report accuracy, per-class F1, pooled macro-F1, an item-level percentile bootstrap interval with 2,000 resamples, and the mean and sample standard deviation of fold macro-F1. Pooled F1 is not an arithmetic mean of fold F1. The bootstrap conditions on saved predictions; it does not measure retraining variability or label uncertainty. A corrected analysis should account for repeated source groups when resampling.

Multimodal improvement is assessed with paired predictions for identical items under identical outer folds. We fixed the comparisons before seeing results: each vision--language model against two text references, TF-IDF/logistic regression and BanglaBERT. For each pair we report the difference in pooled macro-F1 and a percentile interval from 2,000 bootstrap resamples of source groups rather than items, so items sharing a source are resampled together. The paired analysis is repeated at each of five seeds; we report how many seeds yield a bootstrap interval entirely above or below zero. Outlet- or event-held-out evaluation is not performed.

\subsection{Relation to historical scores}
The historical summary reported BanglaBERT at 0.533 on 198 items (Table~\ref{tab:text_models}); the corrected run gives 0.366. % source: reports/submission/matched.json
The two runs differ in several ways at once. The corrected run selects checkpoints without the outer test fold, trains on roughly 80\% of each outer training partition, and fixes the input to the headline field with repaired source groups. We do not attribute the drop to any single change. Checkpoint selection is itself noisy here: inner validation partitions contain 25 items on the matched population and 33 on all headlines, and early stopping ended 64 of 75 neural fold runs, 31 of them at epoch 1 or 2. % source: reports/submission/early_stopping.json (scripts/early_stopping_blipfix.py: published log with the corrected BLIP section)
The fine-tuned encoders are therefore likely under-trained relative to what a larger validation set would select; this run does not test that.

\section{Discussion}
The audit changes what the evidence can support. First, direct overlap with BanglaBias requires attribution and a clear distinction between inherited article data and any additional image collection or annotations. The 53 unmatched rows and annotation procedures still require documentation before a resource-novelty claim.

Second, the matched rerun does not support a multimodal advantage over the best headline-only classifier, and this holds across five seeds (Section~\ref{sec:seeds}). TF-IDF is the top model at every seed; the vision--language deficit against it is large enough that bootstrap intervals exclude zero in most seeds. The comparison with fine-tuned BanglaBERT is weaker in both directions: CLIP exceeds it on average and reliably in 2 of 5 seeds, and the rest are not reliably different. The ablations explain part of this pattern (Section~\ref{sec:ablations}). Only CLIP uses the photograph, and five of the six vision--language models give the same scores with a blank image, so their results reflect how they encode the headline. The photographs do carry label information, as the ViT-only result shows, but neither late fusion with TF-IDF nor most frozen vision--language backbones exploit it. Two further reasons are compatible with this and are not separated here: the vision--language text encoders were not pretrained for Bangla, and 149 items may be too few to learn a useful image--text interaction with frozen backbones. The modality-label disagreement (72 of 152 items) shows that annotators sometimes read the photograph differently from the text, but these models do not convert that difference into better prediction of the final item label.

Third, a common cross-validation label is insufficient to establish comparability. Every historical text score is higher than its corrected all-headline counterpart (Tables~\ref{tab:text_models} and~\ref{tab:all_headlines}). Removing test-informed checkpoint selection and equalizing populations changed both the absolute scores and the ranking, with a linear baseline moving from second place to first.

Finally, the ablations show that photographs are not merely decorative for this label, but they do not establish why only CLIP uses them, or whether tokenization of Bangla script limits the vision--language text encoders; those questions need direct diagnostics. The historical zero-shot LLM summary lacks a matched item population and verified response provenance; it is omitted.

\section{Conclusion}
We audited the lineage and evaluation of a Bangla news stance collection containing reused BanglaBias items and image-associated records, and reran thirteen models under one corrected, matched protocol across five seeds. The annotation tables show uneven annotator coverage and substantial disagreement between modality-specific labels. The historical experiments contained augmentation leakage, outer-test checkpoint selection, and unequal populations. Under the corrected protocol, a character n-gram headline classifier (mean macro-F1 0.524 across seeds) outperforms every vision--language model at every seed. Image ablations show that only CLIP uses the photograph, and even with it CLIP stays below the headline-only classifier.

\section*{Limitations}
The sample is small and imbalanced, with 39 outlet keys and uneven image availability. Five seeds measure fold-assignment and initialisation variability, but hyperparameters were taken from committed configurations without tuning; other learning rates, unfrozen vision--language backbones, or larger validation sets could change individual scores. Inner validation sets of 25--33 items make checkpoint selection noisy, as reflected in seed standard deviations up to 0.060. The paired comparisons with text models confound image use with text representation; the image ablations isolate the photograph only within each model, and a blank or shuffled image tests whether a model uses the photograph, not what it uses it for. Exact lineage for unmatched items, the intended stance target in different political periods, image-collection procedures, annotation instructions, adjudication, consent, and compensation need original study records. The removal of two raw items and several metadata exceptions remain unresolved.

Source-URL grouping alone does not eliminate shared event, outlet, or visual information. Readable-image availability can be selective. Item-bootstrap intervals in Tables~\ref{tab:matched} and~\ref{tab:all_headlines} do not represent training or annotation uncertainty. Outlet- or event-held-out evaluation was not performed.

\section*{Ethical Considerations}
The labels concern annotated framing of individual items, not factual correctness or a normative assessment of a publisher. Political stance is context-sensitive, and disagreement should be retained and documented rather than hidden by an assumed adjudication rule. The measured pairwise agreement does not establish representative annotator viewpoints or absence of bias.

The upstream BanglaBias dataset must be credited. Its dataset card lists an MIT license, but that does not establish permissions for added third-party photographs, recovered article content, or new annotations. Any release should distinguish code, annotations, source links, and copyrighted content, and document their respective terms.
