\begin{abstract}
Evaluating political stance in Bangla news requires distinguishing item-level framing from outlet-level bias and controlling the evidence available to models. We examine a working collection of 198 news items with government-critique, neutral, and government-leaning labels. A provenance audit identifies 145 items with consistent headline or source-link matches to BanglaBias after one verified URL correction, and 53 unmatched items. Separate text and image stance fields agree on 72 of 152 jointly labelled items. We audit historical text and vision--language evaluations and identify augmentation leakage in the original fixed text split, test-fold checkpoint selection in a subsequent cross-validation implementation, and unequal evaluation populations. We retain earlier model summaries as exploratory evidence and specify an evaluation protocol with source grouping, independent inner validation, and matched text--image populations. The available results do not yet establish a reliable multimodal advantage. This analysis clarifies the data lineage and experimental controls needed for a defensible comparison.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
A headline and its accompanying photograph may frame the same political event differently. Predicting that framing requires a defined stance target, a clear account of annotation evidence, and a comparison that holds the evaluation population constant. We study individual news items labelled as government-critique, neutral, or government-leaning. These categories describe annotated framing; they do not determine factual truth, misinformation, or an outlet's enduring political orientation.

Bangla political stance resources already exist. In particular, BanglaBias contains 200 news articles with the same three categories \citep{lia2025read}. Our working collection incorporates items from that resource and includes additional image metadata and modality-specific label fields. Its contribution must therefore be distinguished from the original collection and article annotations.

The available model experiments span several evaluation protocols. The original text split was drawn after augmentation, while original baseline vision--language models and fusion models were evaluated on different populations. A later implementation introduced pooled cross-validation scores but used the outer test fold to select neural checkpoints. These issues affect the interpretation of model rankings and gains.

This revision makes three contributions supported by the audit: a row-level provenance audit, a descriptive analysis of annotation coverage and modality-label disagreement, and an audit of the experimental lineage with a corrected evaluation specification. Historical model summaries are preserved as exploratory results. We do not claim a new first-of-its-kind dataset, a verified fusion advantage, or completed neural runs under the corrected protocol.

\section{Related Work}
\paragraph{Bangla political stance.}
\citet{lia2025read} introduce BanglaBias and evaluate political stance at article level. This resource is part of the lineage of the present collection, rather than solely a comparator. Reusing an article label with only its headline as model input evaluates prediction of that label from a shorter view; it does not establish independently annotated headline-level ground truth. Political stance detection has also been studied in Hindi news \citep{agrawal2022hindi}.

\paragraph{Bangla and multimodal representations.}
BanglaBERT provides a monolingual pretrained representation for Bangla language understanding \citep{bhattacharjee2022banglabert}. Vision--language models offer a possible route to combining a headline and photograph, but the presence of multiple modalities does not establish their incremental usefulness. Multimodal fake-news research surveys cross-modal interaction methods \citep{li2025survey}; our stance target is distinct from veracity detection. We evaluate the conditions needed to compare text-only and multimodal systems on this collection.

The processed table contains 198 items from 39 normalized outlet keys. Examples of outlets include BBC Bangla, Prothom Alo, Jugantor, Samakal, and The Daily Star. The raw annotation sheet contains 200 rows and explicitly records an ``id from bangla bias'' field. Annotators~2 and~3 each cover 197 items and have Cohen's $\kappa=0.731$ on their 197-item overlap. Annotator~1 covers 32 items; pairwise kappas against annotators~2 and~3 are 0.454 and 0.196 on 32-item overlaps. Text/article and image stance labels agree on 72 of 152 jointly labelled items (47.4\%). This describes disagreement between stored modality-specific labels. It may reflect different framing or annotation ambiguity and does not, by itself, establish a benefit from adding images to a classifier.

\subsection{Text and image availability}
The original preprocessed text consists of short headlines, with a median of eight words. The current processed text column mixes 127 recovered article bodies and 71 headlines. The primary corrected comparison fixes the input to the headline field for every model, avoiding source-dependent article-recovery differences. Until annotation records specify otherwise, this is described as predicting item stance from headlines rather than newly annotating headline stance.

The current corpus has 150 image-path entries, of which 149 decode successfully in the audited snapshot. Earlier vision--language summaries use 86 items, while a restricted re-score of text predictions uses 87. These figures refer to different snapshots or eligibility rules; they are not interchangeable descriptions of the dataset. The corrected comparison must freeze one readable-image item list. Image loading converts readable files to RGB and resizes according to model configuration. The present artifacts do not establish perceptual-hash deduplication, a minimum-resolution exclusion rule, or aspect-ratio-preserving padding for every run.

\section{Methods and Evaluation Lineage}\label{sec:methods}
\subsection{Model families}
The saved comparison includes five text encoders (BanglaBERT, Bangla-ELECTRA, mBERT, XLM-RoBERTa, and mT5), a TF-IDF/logistic-regression baseline, and a majority baseline. The text baseline uses character-within-word 2--5-grams, minimum document frequency~2, up to 50,000 features, and logistic regression with $C=1$ and balanced class weights. Vectorization is fitted on training data. The majority floor uses original training-label frequencies without augmentation.

The vision--language families are CLIP, ALIGN, BLIP, ViLT, FLAVA, and a CountVectorizer/ViT hybrid. In the inspected implementation, the hybrid concatenates count-vector text features with frozen ViT representations and fits logistic regression, rather than the SVM described in the previous manuscript. Optimizers, encoder freezing, image sizes, learning rates, and epoch budgets vary by configuration; a single training recipe does not describe all models. For example, the saved BanglaBERT configuration specifies eight epochs, batch size~16, learning rate $2\times10^{-5}$, and early-stopping patience~3.

\subsection{Historical evaluation protocols}
The original text predictions contain 47 test rows from a fixed split of the augmented corpus. Of these, 20 are augmented rows, and 25 source articles are shared between training and test. Thus, the original text scores cannot establish generalization to unseen source articles. Original baseline vision--language predictions cover 30 items from a fixed split, while the original proposed fusion notebook uses five folds over 149 image-associated items. An accuracy of 63.2\% from that fusion notebook cannot establish superiority over a baseline scored on a separate 30-item split.

A later cross-validation implementation reports pooled out-of-fold predictions and fold-level variation. However, its training wrapper supplies the outer test fold as both validation and test data. The neural trainer selects the best checkpoint using validation macro-F1, so this exposes the outer evaluation labels to checkpoint selection. Its grouping also falls back to individual item IDs when the requested source-group column is absent. The corpus lacks that column, and individual item IDs do not protect the repeated source URLs. We have repaired the shared split logic to join declared groups, normalized URLs, and augmentation parents transitively; the historical neural runs still require rerunning. Tables~\ref{tab:text_models} and~\ref{tab:multimodal_models} retain the later saved summaries with these qualifications; they are not corrected-protocol results.

\subsection{Corrected comparison protocol}\label{sec:experimental_setup}
The required primary comparison uses the same original readable-image items, headline inputs, source groups, and outer folds for every text-only and multimodal model. Repeated source URLs must share a group; metadata conflicts require correction or conservative exclusion before the item list is frozen. Evaluation retains the natural label distribution.

Within each outer training partition, a separate grouped validation partition selects neural checkpoints. The outer test partition is used only for final prediction. Any augmented headline is attached only if its parent belongs to the inner training partition. The published augmentation table contains 114 variants; neither validation nor test data should contain these variants. The code now provides independent inner validation, but source-group repair and corrected neural reruns remain pending. We do not present this specification as an already completed experiment.

Every eligible original item must receive exactly one out-of-fold prediction. Runs should retain item IDs, fold assignments, model configuration, seed, code revision, and input provenance. A secondary all-headline evaluation can cover all 198 items, but its scores belong in a separate population table.

\subsection{Metrics and uncertainty}
The primary metric is macro-F1 over the three fixed labels. We report accuracy, per-class F1, pooled macro-F1, an item-level percentile bootstrap interval with 2,000 resamples, and the mean and sample standard deviation of fold macro-F1. Pooled F1 is not an arithmetic mean of fold F1. The bootstrap conditions on saved predictions; it does not measure retraining variability or label uncertainty. A corrected analysis should account for repeated source groups when resampling.

An assessment of multimodal improvement requires paired predictions for identical items under identical training partitions, followed by a paired estimate of the macro-F1 difference. Overlap or non-overlap of separate confidence intervals is not a substitute for that analysis. Multiple-seed and outlet- or event-held-out evaluation would address additional generalization questions, but those experiments are not claimed here.

\subsection{Text and vision--language summaries}
The saved text summary has $n=198$ and a BanglaBERT point estimate of macro-F1~0.533. TF-IDF/logistic regression and mBERT score 0.501 and 0.498. These figures motivate retaining a linear baseline in future comparisons, but they do not establish a robust ranking or identify the causal effect of language-specific pretraining.

The vision--language summary covers $n=86$ and has CLIP and ALIGN point estimates of 0.516 and 0.482. The wide intervals and large fold variation indicate considerable conditional uncertainty. These values cannot be compared directly with the full-corpus text scores. Class-level zeros, such as FLAVA's government-leaning F1, describe failures in those saved predictions; they do not establish a limitation inherent to an architecture.

\subsection{Restricted re-scoring and ablations}
The earlier restricted text re-score includes 87 image-associated items, compared with 86 in the vision--language table. It filters predictions from text models trained on the full-corpus folds and therefore changes neither their training population nor their checkpoint-selection procedure. Table~\ref{tab:text_on_image_subset} in Appendix~\ref{sec:historical} preserves this descriptive re-score without claiming a matched comparison.

Appendix~\ref{sec:historical} also retains the historical ablation point estimates. Equal rounded scores with and without augmentation do not establish that augmentation has no effect; positive differences after unfreezing encoders do not establish reliable improvement. Those conclusions require verified input/configuration changes and paired reruns. Previously quoted significance tests and aggregate error counts are not used as final evidence because the underlying evaluation issues remain unresolved.

\subsection{Fresh headline baseline diagnostics}
A corrected local headline-only diagnostic gives TF-IDF macro-F1~0.496 (95\% item-bootstrap interval 0.415--0.569, $n=198$) and 0.543 (0.457--0.619, $n=149$ readable-image items). Its corresponding original-frequency majority floors are 0.228 and 0.217. These reruns use repaired source grouping and the verified URL correction; augmentation is attached only to training parents. The difference between their two TF-IDF scores is a population difference, not an image benefit: both classifiers use text alone.

\section{Discussion}
The audit changes what the evidence can support. First, direct overlap with BanglaBias requires attribution and a clear distinction between inherited article data and any additional image collection or annotations. The 53 unmatched rows and annotation procedures still require documentation before a resource-novelty claim.

Second, modality-label disagreement is an informative descriptive property, but it is not a performance result. It motivates investigating when text and image views diverge while leaving open whether the disagreement is reliable, relevant to the final item label, and learnable from this sample.

Third, a common cross-validation label is insufficient to establish comparability. The same eligibility list, source grouping, fold assignment, checkpoint-selection boundary, and annotation target are needed. Subset re-scoring and confidence intervals do not repair unequal training populations or test-informed checkpoint selection.

Finally, claims that tokenizers render Bangla text unusable, photographs are merely decorative, or a particular fusion architecture explains a gain require direct diagnostics or controlled ablations. The available aggregate results do not establish those mechanisms. The historical zero-shot LLM summary also lacks a matched item population and independently verified response/model provenance in this audit; it is omitted from the main comparison rather than used to explain task difficulty.

\section{Conclusion}
We audited the lineage and evaluation of a Bangla news stance collection containing reused BanglaBias items and image-associated records. The annotation tables show uneven annotator coverage and substantial disagreement between modality-specific labels. The experimental record reveals augmentation leakage in the original text split, outer-test checkpoint selection in a later cross-validation implementation, and unequal model populations. Correcting these controls is necessary before claiming a reliable multimodal gain. The next experimental stage is a matched, source-grouped comparison with independent validation and saved out-of-fold predictions.

\section*{Limitations}
This is a revision-stage audit with exploratory model summaries, not a completed corrected benchmark. The sample is small and imbalanced, with 39 outlet keys and uneven image availability. Exact lineage for unmatched items, the intended stance target in different political periods, image-collection procedures, annotation instructions, adjudication, consent, and compensation need original study records. The removal of two raw items and several metadata exceptions remain unresolved.

Source-URL grouping alone does not eliminate shared event, outlet, or visual information. Readable-image availability can be selective, and article recovery is source-dependent. Item-bootstrap intervals do not represent training or annotation uncertainty. A definitive negative result about multimodal learning cannot be drawn from unmatched or test-selected historical experiments.

\section*{Ethical Considerations}
The labels concern annotated framing of individual items, not factual correctness or a normative assessment of a publisher. Political stance is context-sensitive, and disagreement should be retained and documented rather than hidden by an assumed adjudication rule. The measured pairwise agreement does not establish representative annotator viewpoints or absence of bias.

The upstream BanglaBias dataset must be credited. Its dataset card lists an MIT license, but that does not establish permissions for added third-party photographs, recovered article content, or new annotations. Any release should distinguish code, annotations, source links, and copyrighted content, and document their respective terms. We do not claim that publication of news content establishes consent or unrestricted reuse. Automated stance predictions should not be treated as evidence sufficient for censorship, political targeting, or publisher sanctions.
